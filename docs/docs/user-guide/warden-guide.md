# Warden Guide

Welcome, Warden! You've been invited to help someone manage their digital wellbeing. This guide explains your role, responsibilities, and how to use the warden dashboard effectively.

## Understanding Your Role

### What is a Warden?

A warden is a trusted friend or family member who provides accountability for someone using CellBlock (the "inmate"). You act as a safeguard against impulsive decisions and help enforce healthy digital boundaries.

### Your Responsibilities

As a warden, you:

- **Review and approve/deny requests** for whitelist additions and time budget changes
- **Grant emergency time** (parole) when genuinely needed
- **Trigger lockdowns** if agreements are violated or emergencies arise
- **Monitor usage patterns** to identify concerning trends
- **Provide support and encouragement** for digital wellbeing goals

### What You Can See

You have access to:

- Current time remaining (real-time)
- Daily and weekly usage totals
- Usage history (last month + current month)
- All pending requests with context
- Active devices and their status
- Other wardens for the same inmate
- All approval/denial decisions from other wardens
- Parole grants and lockdown history

### What You Cannot See

To protect privacy, you cannot see:

- Specific websites visited
- Specific apps used (except those in whitelist requests)
- Content consumed
- Messages or communications
- Real-time device screenshots or monitoring

!!! info "Privacy-First Design"
    CellBlock tracks time usage, not content. You see patterns and totals, not browsing history.

## Accepting Invitations

### When You're Invited

You'll receive an email invitation from someone asking you to be their warden:

**Email contains:**

- Who invited you
- Personal message (if they included one)
- What being a warden entails
- Accept/Decline links

### Before Accepting

Consider these questions:

1. **Do you know this person well?**
    - Best wardens are friends, family, roommates, or partners
    - You should understand their goals and struggles

2. **Can you commit to responding?**
    - Inmates may need timely responses (within hours, not days)
    - Requests expire after 3 days if unanswered

3. **Are you comfortable with this responsibility?**
    - You'll need to make judgment calls
    - Sometimes you'll need to deny requests
    - This requires firmness and compassion

4. **Do you have your own healthy relationship with technology?**
    - Hard to enforce limits you don't respect yourself
    - Consider your own digital wellbeing

### How to Accept

1. Click **Accept Invitation** in the email
2. Create a CellBlock account (or log in if you have one)
3. Read the warden responsibilities overview
4. Click **Confirm Acceptance**

**What happens next:**

- Inmate receives notification that you accepted
- You gain access to their warden dashboard
- All future whitelist/budget changes require your approval
- You start receiving notifications for their requests

### How to Decline

1. Click **Decline Invitation** in the email
2. Optionally add a message explaining why:

    Example:
    > "I appreciate you thinking of me, but I'm not sure I can commit to responding quickly enough. Maybe ask Sarah instead?"

3. Click **Confirm Decline**

**What happens:**

- Inmate receives notification that you declined
- Your message (if any) is sent to them
- They can invite someone else

- Approve/deny pending requests

#### Real-Time Status

## Approving Requests

Inmates send requests for whitelist additions and time budget changes. You review and approve or deny each request.

### Types of Requests

#### Whitelist Addition Request

Inmate wants to add an app or website to their whitelist:

**You see:**

- App name / domain
- Platform (iOS/Windows)
- Inmate's comment explaining why they need it
- Current whitelist items for context

**Evaluate:**

- Is this truly necessary?
- Is it a work tool, educational resource, or essential service?
- Or is it entertainment/distraction disguised as utility?
- Does their explanation make sense?

**Examples:**

Good request:
> "Need to add zoom.us for daily team standups at 9 AM. I'm fully remote and can't join meetings without this."
> **Approve** - Legitimate work requirement

Questionable request:
> "Want to add netflix.com so I can watch documentaries for learning"
> **Deny** - Entertainment disguised as education

Edge case:
> "Need to add reddit.com because my programming questions on r/learnpython get answered there"
> **Request More Info** - Could be legitimate, but risky

#### Time Budget Change Request

Inmate wants to increase their daily or weekly limits:

**You see:**

- Current limits
- Proposed new limits
- Difference (e.g., +30 minutes per day)
- Inmate's comment explaining why
- Recent usage patterns

**Evaluate:**

- Is this a temporary need or permanent increase?
- Have they been consistently hitting their current limit?
- Is the reason legitimate (new commitment, changed schedule)?
- Or are they just wanting more time for entertainment?

**Examples:**

Good request:
> "Starting online MBA program requiring 90 min/day of coursework. Need to increase weekday limit from 120→180 for next 16 weeks"
> **Approve** - Temporary, educational, specific duration

Bad request:
> "I keep running out of time and it's annoying. Need more."
> **Deny** - No legitimate reason, just wanting to reduce friction

Temporary situation:
> "Big project deadline this Friday. Need +60 min per day Mon-Thu this week only."
> **Approve with comment** - "Approved for this week only. Let's discuss if this becomes a pattern."

### How to Approve

1. Go to **Requests** tab in dashboard
2. Click on the pending request
3. Read the inmate's comment carefully
4. Check recent usage patterns (are they consistently hitting limits?)
5. Click **Approve**
6. Optionally add a comment:

    Example:
    > "Approved because this is clearly work-related. Let's check in next month to see if you still need it."

7. Click **Confirm Approval**

**What happens:**

- Change applies immediately
- Inmate receives email and push notification
- Change logged in history

### How to Deny

1. Review the request (same steps as approving)
2. Click **Deny**
3. **Add a comment explaining why** (strongly recommended):

    Example:
    > "I don't think Instagram is necessary for your digital wellbeing goals. We agreed to keep social media off the whitelist. Let's talk about this if you still think you need it."

4. Click **Confirm Denial**

**What happens:**

- Request marked as denied
- Inmate receives notification with your comment
- No changes applied
- Logged in history

!!! tip "Always Explain Denials"
    Denying without explanation can feel arbitrary and damage trust. Take 30 seconds to write why you're denying. This helps the inmate understand your reasoning.

### Request More Information

If you need clarification before deciding:

1. Click **Request More Info**
2. Ask specific questions:

    Example:
    > "Can you explain more about why you need Reddit specifically? Are there alternative resources for programming help that wouldn't include distractions?"

3. Click **Send**

**What happens:**

- Inmate receives your message
- They can respond with additional context
- Request remains pending
- You'll be notified when they respond

### Bulk Actions

To approve/deny multiple requests at once:

1. Go to **Requests** tab
2. Check boxes next to requests
3. Click **Approve Selected** or **Deny Selected**
4. Add comment (applied to all selected)
5. Confirm

!!! warning "Use Bulk Actions Carefully"
    Review each request individually before bulk approving. Don't approve blindly just to clear the queue.

## Granting Parole

Parole is emergency time you grant when an inmate has run out of time but genuinely needs access.

### When to Grant Parole

**Appropriate situations:**

- Legitimate work emergency or deadline
- Educational requirement (assignment due, exam)
- Family emergency requiring coordination
- Medical situation requiring research
- Travel disruption requiring rebooking
- One-time special event (watching a live event, family video call)

**Inappropriate situations:**

- Ran out of time watching entertainment (let them wait until tomorrow)
- "Bored" or "nothing else to do"
- Didn't plan well and now wants to cram entertainment
- Trying to work around limits consistently (pattern of abuse)

### Types of Parole

#### Time-Based Parole

Add specific number of minutes to their budget:

**Use when:**

- They need a defined amount of time for a specific task
- Example: "60 minutes to finish work presentation"

**How to grant:**

1. Go to inmate's dashboard
2. Click **Grant Parole**
3. Select **Time-Based**
4. Enter minutes (e.g., 60)
5. Add comment explaining why:

    Example:
    > "Granted 60 min for work deadline. This is a one-time exception. Plan better next time."

6. Click **Grant**

#### Deadline-Based Parole

Unlock until specific date/time:

**Use when:**

- Open-ended time needed
- Multi-day exception (traveling, conference, etc.)
- Example: "Unlock until end of day Friday"

**How to grant:**

1. Click **Grant Parole**
2. Select **Deadline-Based**
3. Pick date and time (in inmate's timezone)
4. Add comment
5. Click **Grant**

!!! info "Parole Replacement"
    If you grant new parole while previous parole is active, new one replaces old one. They don't stack.

### Parole Best Practices

1. **Make it clear this is exceptional**
    - Don't grant parole routinely
    - If granting weekly, their time limits are too low

2. **Be specific in comments**
    - Explain exactly why you're granting
    - Set expectations for future

3. **Track patterns**
    - If same person requests parole frequently, have a conversation
    - Adjust base time limits if needed

4. **Deny when appropriate**
    - It's okay to say no
    - Poor planning on their part doesn't constitute an emergency on yours

### Denying Parole Requests

Inmates can request parole, and you can deny:

1. Go to **Requests** tab
2. Find parole request
3. Read their explanation
4. Click **Deny**
5. Add comment explaining why:

    Example:
    > "This sounds like you want more time to watch YouTube, which isn't an emergency. You have whitelisted apps available. See you tomorrow when your budget resets."

6. Click **Confirm Denial**

## Triggering Lockdowns

Lockdown immediately sets an inmate's time to 0, forcing them into lockdown mode.

### When to Trigger Lockdown

**Appropriate situations:**

- Inmate violated an agreement
- You observe concerning usage patterns (spending entire daily budget in one sitting)
- Emergency requiring them to focus (family crisis, work crisis)
- Requested by inmate themselves (self-imposed digital detox)
- Inmate is gaming the system (device hopping, etc.)

**Inappropriate situations:**

- You're annoyed with them for unrelated reasons
- Punishment without prior agreement
- They're using time normally within their limits
- You disagree with how they spend their time (but they're within limits)

!!! warning "Lockdown is Serious"
    Only use lockdown for serious situations. It immediately cuts off their access and should be discussed beforehand or explained clearly afterward.

### Immediate Lockdown

Cut off access right now:

1. Go to inmate's dashboard
2. Click **Trigger Lockdown**
3. Select **Immediate**
4. Add comment explaining why (required):

    Example:
    > "You violated our agreement by trying to remove me as warden. Immediate lockdown until we can talk about this."

5. Type "LOCKDOWN" to confirm
6. Click **Confirm**

**What happens:**

- All devices immediately locked
- Time remaining set to 0
- Inmate receives notification with your comment
- Logged in history

### Lockdown with Grace Period

Give them warning before lockdown:

1. Click **Trigger Lockdown**
2. Select **Grace Period**
3. Enter grace period in minutes (e.g., 30)
4. Add comment:

    Example:
    > "You've used 3 hours in the first 3 hours of the day. Lockdown in 30 minutes to give you time to wrap up. Let's discuss better pacing."

5. Click **Confirm**

**What happens:**

- Inmate receives notification with countdown
- Timer displayed in their app
- After grace period expires, lockdown engages
- Logged in history

### After Lockdown

Inmates remain locked until:

- Their daily budget resets at midnight, OR
- You grant parole, OR
- They break glass (ends your relationship)

**Follow-up communication:**

- Send message explaining what needs to change
- Discuss whether time limits need adjustment
- Clarify agreements and expectations
- Offer support, not just punishment

## Viewing Usage Reports

Monitor your inmates' usage patterns to identify trends and provide support.

### Daily Usage View

See today's usage:

1. Go to inmate's dashboard
2. View **Today** tab
3. See:
    - Total time used so far
    - Time remaining
    - Usage timeline (when they used time)
    - Active devices

### Weekly Report

View past 7 days:

1. Go to **Weekly** tab
2. See:
    - Bar chart of daily usage
    - Days over limit (highlighted in red)
    - Total weekly usage vs limit
    - Compliance rate
    - Average daily usage

**Look for patterns:**

- Consistently hitting limit = may need higher limits
- Large variance (some days 30 min, some days 240 min) = poor planning
- Weekend spikes = normal for many people
- Weekday spikes = possible procrastination

### Monthly Trends

View longer-term patterns:

1. Go to **Monthly** tab
2. See:
    - Line graph of weekly totals
    - Trend direction (improving/worsening)
    - Best week and worst week
    - Comparison to previous months

### Monthly Summary Email

You'll receive a monthly email summary:

**Email contains:**

- Usage highlights for each inmate
- Compliance rates
- Notable changes vs previous month
- Link to full dashboard

**Action items:**

- Review inmates who are struggling
- Send encouragement to those improving
- Reach out to discuss patterns or adjustments

### What to Look For

#### Red Flags

- **Consistently maxing out daily limit within first few hours**
    - May indicate binge behavior
    - Consider conversation about pacing

- **Frequent parole requests (multiple per week)**
    - Base limits may be too low
    - Or poor planning

- **Usage patterns mirror stress** (spike during known stressful periods)
    - Normal and expected
    - Offer support

- **Declining trend** (using less time each week)
    - Positive! Acknowledge progress

#### Green Flags

- **Consistent usage below limits**
    - Healthy habits forming

- **Decreasing parole requests**
    - Learning to work within limits

- **Stable patterns**
    - Predictable usage = good planning

## Working with Multiple Wardens

Inmates can have up to 4 wardens (1 primary + 3 backups). Here's how this works:

### Warden Visibility

You can see:

- All other wardens for the same inmate
- Their names and emails
- Who approved/denied which requests
- Comments left by other wardens
- Parole grants from other wardens

### Any Warden Can Approve

Only one warden needs to approve a request:

- If you approve, request is granted immediately
- Other wardens are notified
- If another warden approved first, you see it marked as "Approved by [Name]"

!!! tip "Coordinate with Other Wardens"
    Consider creating a shared chat or email thread with other wardens to discuss major decisions and stay aligned.

### Primary vs Backup

**Primary Warden:**

- Receives all notifications first
- Listed first in inmate's warden list
- No special privileges beyond that

**Backup Wardens:**

- Full access to same dashboard and powers
- Serve as redundancy if primary is unavailable
- Automatically promoted to primary if primary resigns

### Disagreeing with Other Wardens

If another warden approved something you would have denied:

1. Don't undo it (creates inconsistency)
2. Add a comment expressing your perspective:

    Example:
    > "I see Sarah approved this, but I want to note I'm concerned about Twitter being whitelisted. Let's discuss as a warden team."

3. Reach out to other wardens to align
4. Discuss with inmate if pattern continues

## Communication Best Practices

### Be Clear and Direct

Good comment:
> "Denied because Twitter is social media and we agreed to keep social media off your whitelist. I know you miss it, but this is what you asked me to enforce."

Vague comment:
> "No"

### Be Supportive, Not Punitive

Good approach:
> "I'm granting this parole, but I'm concerned about the pattern. You've needed emergency time 3 times this week. Let's talk about whether your base limits are too low or if something else is going on."

Punitive approach:
> "Fine, but this is the last time."

### Respond Promptly

- Aim to respond within 24 hours
- Requests expire after 3 days
- Timely responses show you care

### Encourage Progress

When you see improvement:

> "I noticed you've been under your limit 9 out of the last 10 days! Great work. Keep it up!"

### Be Willing to Adjust

If they consistently need more time:

> "You've requested parole 4 times in two weeks. Maybe we should increase your base daily limit from 90 to 120 minutes? Let's discuss."

## Resigning as Warden

If you can no longer be a warden:

### How to Resign

1. Go to **My Inmates** dashboard
2. Click on the inmate
3. Click **Resign as Warden**
4. Add a message explaining why (optional):

    Example:
    > "I'm starting a new job and won't have time to respond quickly to requests. I think you'd be better served by someone more available. You're doing great, keep it up!"

5. Click **Confirm Resignation**

**What happens:**

- You immediately lose access to their dashboard
- Inmate receives notification
- Their backup warden is automatically promoted to primary
- If no backup, they enter "no warden" mode

### Before Resigning

Consider:

1. **Discuss with inmate first**
    - Don't surprise them
    - Help them identify a replacement
    - Offer to help transition

2. **Coordinate with other wardens**
    - Ensure someone else is available
    - Share any context or concerns

3. **Suggest replacement**
    - Recommend someone who'd be good
    - Offer to reach out to potential new warden

!!! info "No Penalty"
    Resigning is completely acceptable. Better to resign than to be an unresponsive warden.

## Handling Difficult Situations

### Inmate is Angry About Denials

**Scenario:** Inmate is upset that you denied their request.

**Response:**

1. Acknowledge their frustration
2. Restate why you denied
3. Remind them this is what they asked you to do
4. Offer to discuss via call/in-person if tension high

Example message:
> "I know you're frustrated, and I get it. But when you invited me to be your warden, you asked me to help you reduce phone time, and that's what I'm doing. Let's chat on the phone tonight to talk this through."

### Inmate Keeps Requesting Same Thing

**Scenario:** You denied a request, but they re-request it repeatedly.

**Response:**

1. Deny again
2. Explain that re-requesting won't change your answer
3. State what would need to change for you to approve
4. Consider whether limits need adjustment

Example:
> "This is the third time you've requested Instagram. My answer hasn't changed. If you think you genuinely need social media for your work/life, let's have a conversation about that, not just keep requesting."

### Inmate Gaming the System

**Scenario:** You notice inmate is device-hopping, using "utility" apps to access blocked content, or other workarounds.

**Response:**

1. Trigger lockdown if egregious
2. Have direct conversation
3. Remind them of their goals
4. Decide together whether CellBlock is right for them

Example:
> "I noticed you've been using the browser in your banking app to access YouTube. That's defeating the purpose. If you don't want to do this anymore, you can break glass, but don't ask me to be your warden if you're just going to work around the system."

### You Disagree with Requests but They Seem Legitimate

**Scenario:** Inmate requests something that technically fits criteria but you have a gut feeling it's not right.

**Response:**

1. Request more information
2. Discuss with other wardens
3. Approve with conditions
4. Monitor closely

Example:
> "I'm approving Reddit for programming help, but I want to check in next week. If I see you're spending tons of time on it, we'll revisit this decision."

### Inmate Breaks Glass

**Scenario:** Inmate activates break glass, ending your warden relationship.

**Response:**

1. You receive notification immediately
2. Read their comment (if any) to understand why
3. Reach out personally (not through CellBlock) to check in
4. Don't take it personally - they may have good reason
5. Offer to be warden again if/when they return

Example message:
> "Hey, I saw you broke glass on CellBlock. No judgment - just wanted to check if everything is okay. If you decide to use it again and want me as warden, I'm happy to help. Take care."

## Being a Great Warden

### Core Principles

1. **Enforce with compassion**
    - You're helping, not controlling
    - Be firm but kind

2. **Communicate openly**
    - Explain your decisions
    - Be available for conversations

3. **Respect their autonomy**
    - This is their journey, you're supporting
    - They can always break glass if they want out

4. **Be consistent**
    - Don't approve something today you'd deny tomorrow
    - Set clear expectations

5. **Monitor without micromanaging**
    - Check in regularly
    - Don't obsess over every detail

6. **Celebrate progress**
    - Acknowledge improvements
    - Encourage positive trends

### Red Flags You Should Watch For (In Yourself)

- **Approving everything to avoid conflict**
    - You're not helping them
    - They can just remove you as warden if you're rubber-stamping

- **Denying everything rigidly**
    - Balance is key
    - Some flexibility is needed for life changes

- **Using warden powers to control/manipulate**
    - This is abuse of trust
    - Resign if you can't be objective

- **Ignoring requests for days**
    - Respond promptly or resign
    - Unresponsiveness defeats the purpose

### Questions to Ask Yourself

Before approving/denying:

1. "Would I approve this if I were them?"
2. "Does this align with the goals they shared when inviting me?"
3. "Is this a need or a want?"
4. "Am I being consistent with past decisions?"
5. "What would help them most in the long run?"

## Account Settings

### Your Profile

Update your warden profile:

1. Go to **Settings → Profile**
2. Update:
    - Display name
    - Profile picture
    - Contact email
    - Notification preferences

### Notification Preferences

Control which notifications you receive:

1. Go to **Settings → Notifications**
2. Configure:
    - New requests (push + email)
    - Break glass events (push + email)
    - Monthly reports (email only)
    - Device offline warnings (email only)

!!! info "Can't Disable All Notifications"
    Critical notifications (break glass, device uninstall) cannot be disabled. You agreed to monitor when you accepted.

### Deleting Your Account

To delete your warden account:

1. Go to **Settings → Account → Delete Account**
2. Understand consequences:
    - All inmates you supervise are notified
    - They're prompted to promote backup warden
    - You lose access to all warden dashboards
3. Confirm deletion

**Alternative: Resign Instead**

If you want to stop supervising specific inmates but keep your account:

- Resign from each inmate individually
- Keep account for future use
